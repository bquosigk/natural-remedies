Ground Rules for Kids

Read once. These apply to every remedy in this guide.

  1. No honey before the first birthday. Not raw, not cooked, not in syrups, not on a pacifier. Infant botulism is rare but serious. [59] CDC
  2. Babies under 3 months with a fever of 100.4°F (38°C) or more need a doctor right away. Don't wait to try home remedies. [62] AAP HealthyChildren
  3. No aspirin for children or teens with a fever, cold, or flu. It is linked to Reye syndrome. A few old family remedies still recommend it.
  4. Essential oils are concentrated medicines. Don't put oregano, eucalyptus, peppermint, or camphor oils on or near the face of babies and young children, and never give them by mouth. Store them locked up. A small swallow can poison a toddler. [24] Poison Control (poison.org); [73] NIH NCCIH
  5. No menthol or camphor chest rubs under age 2. [15] Abanses 2009, Chest
  6. No steam bowls or kettles with kids. Scald burns from steam inhalation are common. Use a steamy bathroom with the child on your lap instead. [21] Br J Gen Pract 2016
  7. Neti pots and rinses need distilled, sterile, or boiled-and-cooled water. [23] U.S. FDA
  8. Raw garlic is strong medicine for skin. Applied directly, it has caused chemical burns on children. Garlic-in-oil must be refrigerated and used within 4 days. [64] Pediatr Emerg Care 2019; [59] CDC
  9. Elderberries must be cooked. Raw or unripe berries, stems, and leaves can make people sick. [46] NIH NCCIH; [60] CDC MMWR 1984
  10. Over-the-counter cough and cold medicines aren't recommended under age 6, which is one reason honey, saline, and humidity are pediatricians' first choices too. [17] AAP HealthyChildren

Emergency: 911 · Poison Control (Georgia Poison Center): 1-800-222-1222, 24/7

Safety & kids notes by symptom

Sore Throat

  • No honey of any kind for babies under 12 months (infant botulism risk). That includes honey on a pacifier and honey baked into home remedies.
  • Young children usually can't gargle without swallowing, and the NHS says children should not gargle salt water. Offer warm drinks, honey (age 1+), and cold treats instead.
  • Hard candies and lozenges are a choking risk under age 4.
  • Hot-toddy versions with whiskey or brandy are for adults only.

Cough

  • No honey under 12 months. For babies, use saline drops, suction, humid air, and extra feeds instead.
  • No camphor or menthol rubs under age 2. They can increase mucus and airway irritation in very young children, and camphor is toxic if swallowed.
  • The AAP advises against over-the-counter cough and cold medicines under age 6 and cough drops under age 4.
  • For babies under 1, keep the sleep surface flat and bare: no pillows, wedges, or blankets.

Congestion & Sinus

  • Use only distilled, sterile, or boiled-and-cooled tap water for neti pots and rinses, never straight tap water. The FDA links tap-water rinses to rare but deadly amoeba infections.
  • Never use steam bowls or kettles with children, because scald burns are common. Use shower steam or a cool-mist humidifier instead.
  • Essential oils (eucalyptus, peppermint, camphor) should not be put on or near the faces of infants and small children, and must never be swallowed. Keep bottles out of reach.
  • Star anise tea is not safe for babies. Japanese star anise contamination has poisoned infants.

Seasonal Allergies

  • No honey under 12 months. Pollen-allergic people can react to honey, bee pollen, and propolis.
  • Raw butterbur contains liver-toxic pyrrolizidine alkaloids, so use PA-free products only, and never give butterbur to children or use it in pregnancy.
  • Chamomile and echinacea teas can trigger reactions in people with ragweed allergy.
  • Use only distilled or boiled water in rinses.

Common Cold

  • No honey under 12 months, including in elderberry syrup and garlic honey. For babies, make elderberry syrup with maple syrup or skip it.
  • Elderberries must be cooked. Raw or unripe berries, leaves, stems, and bark contain cyanide-producing compounds and caused a mass poisoning in 1983 (CDC). If foraging in Georgia, be certain of identification, use only ripe dark berries, and remove all stems.
  • Garlic in amounts above food levels can increase bleeding risk with blood thinners (warfarin, aspirin, clopidogrel) and may interact with some HIV drugs. NCCIH says garlic supplements may not be safe in pregnancy above food amounts.
  • Homemade garlic-in-oil must be refrigerated and thrown out after 4 days (botulism risk).
  • Little is known about elderberry safety in pregnancy, breastfeeding, or autoimmune conditions.

Fever

  • Never give aspirin to children or teens with a fever or viral illness, because it can cause Reye syndrome. Some older home remedies, including one community post below, recommend it.
  • No alcohol rubs, ice baths, or cold baths. Don't pile on blankets.
  • No raw garlic on skin, which has caused chemical burns.
  • For a baby under 3 months, call the doctor before trying any home treatment.

Earache

  • Never put anything in the ear if there is fluid, pus, or blood draining, if ear tubes are in, or if the eardrum might be torn. Have the eardrum checked first if unsure.
  • Never put cotton swabs, onion pieces, or garlic cloves inside a child's ear canal (injury and choking risk). Use them only outside the ear.
  • Raw garlic oil can sting or burn. Strain it well, and stop if the child complains of burning.
  • Homemade garlic oil must be refrigerated and used within 4 days (botulism).
  • Oil should be body temperature only. Microwaved oil can have hot spots.

Upset Stomach & Nausea

  • Ginger is generally safe. Large supplement doses may add to blood-thinner effects. In pregnancy, food amounts are fine, but check with your provider about supplements.
  • Peppermint oil capsules can cause heartburn. Never apply peppermint oil to infants' faces.
  • Keep rubbing alcohol away from children. It is a sniff-only remedy for adults.

Diarrhea

  • Avoid sugary juice, soda, and sports drinks as the main fluid for small children, because the sugar can worsen diarrhea.
  • Activated charcoal can block absorption of prescription medicines (seizure medicines, birth control, and others). Ask a pharmacist first.
  • Homemade rehydration solution must be measured exactly, because too much salt is dangerous. For babies and toddlers, use store-bought solution.

Constipation

  • No honey or corn syrup for babies under 1, including old "Karo syrup" remedies.
  • Don't give mineral oil by mouth to infants (choking/aspiration risk), and don't give enemas or suppositories to babies without a doctor's advice.
  • Babies under 6 months: ask your pediatrician before giving juice.

Urinary Discomfort (UTI)

  • Cranberry may interact with warfarin (NCCIH). Sweetened juice adds a lot of sugar.
  • Children with any UTI symptoms (or an unexplained fever) need a urine test. Kidney infections in young children can scar the kidneys.

Headache

  • Peppermint oil must be diluted, kept away from the eyes, and never applied to the face of a baby or young child (menthol can cause breathing problems).
  • Watch for an overuse pattern: frequent headaches in a child are worth a doctor visit, whatever the remedy.

Minor Cuts & Burns

  • Don't use butter, oil, ice, toothpaste, or soy sauce on a burn before cooling it with running water.
  • Raw garlic on skin can cause chemical burns, so don't use it on wounds or burns.
  • Honey is for the outside of the body only for babies. Oral honey under 1 is the botulism risk. Many guides still avoid honey on infant wounds, so ask your pediatrician.
  • Aloe latex (the yellow layer under the skin of the leaf) should not be swallowed. Oral aloe may be unsafe in pregnancy.

Rashes & Itch

  • Patch-test new remedies on a small area first. Chamomile and lavender can trigger allergic skin reactions, and lavender oil has been linked to breast swelling in boys.
  • Avoid essential oils on babies' skin and on broken skin.
  • Very hot water may briefly calm itchy bites in adults but can scald children, so don't use it for kids, and never use it on hives.

Insomnia & Restlessness

  • Melatonin is a hormone supplement, not a herb, and gummies often contain more or less than the label says. Ask a pediatrician before using it in children. Store it out of reach, because child overdoses are rising.
  • Chamomile can cause allergic reactions (especially in people allergic to ragweed) and may interact with warfarin.
  • Don't put essential oils in a baby's room or on their skin.

Flu-like Aches

  • Oregano oil is concentrated and can burn skin and mucous membranes. Don't give it to infants or young children, and avoid it in pregnancy. It may also interact with blood thinners.
  • Never give aspirin for flu-like illness to anyone under 18 (Reye syndrome).
  • Keep all essential oils locked up. A teaspoon swallowed can poison a child. Poison Control: 1-800-222-1222.

A gentle note: This is a family reference of traditional home remedies, not medical advice, and it has not been reviewed by a clinician. Many remedies are about comfort while the body heals. None replace a doctor’s care for a seriously ill child, or vaccines and antibiotics for serious infections. When in doubt, call your doctor. Emergency: 911 · Poison Control: 1-800-222-1222.
