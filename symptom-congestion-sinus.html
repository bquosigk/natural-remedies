Nasal Congestion & Sinus

A stuffy nose usually comes with a cold and peaks around days 2–4. Sinus pressure that clears within 10 days is usually viral too.

Traditional remedies

Saline drops, spray, or rinse

Some evidence
How to make & use
Babies and toddlers: 2–3 saline drops in each nostril, wait a minute, then suction gently with a bulb or nasal aspirator before feeds and sleep. Older kids and adults: saline spray, or a squeeze bottle or neti pot with ¼–½ tsp non-iodized salt plus a pinch of baking soda in 1 cup of safe water, once or twice a day.
Tradition
Nasal washing (jala neti) comes from Ayurvedic and yogic practice in India.
What the research says
A Cochrane review found saline irrigation may modestly ease cold symptoms. NIH NCCIH lists nasal rinsing among the approaches that may help. The AAP recommends saline and suction for children. [20] Cochrane review; [4] NIH NCCIH; [10] AAP HealthyChildren

Steam (shower or bowl)

Traditional use only
How to make & use
Sit in a steamy bathroom with the hot shower running for 10–15 minutes, holding small children on your lap. Adults can lean over a bowl of hot water with a towel over the head. Some add a few eucalyptus leaves or 1–2 drops of eucalyptus oil (adults only).
Tradition
Steam inhalation over a basin is an old European and Asian practice.
What the research says
A Cochrane review found no consistent benefit from heated, humidified air for colds, though many people find it comforting. Bowls of hot water cause serious scald burns in children. [16] Cochrane review; [21] Br J Gen Pract 2016

Warm compress

Traditional use only
How to make & use
Lay a warm, damp washcloth across the nose and cheeks for 5–10 minutes, several times a day.
Tradition
Universal home care.
What the research says
A comfort measure with no specific trials. [10] AAP HealthyChildren

Hot, spicy, or brothy foods; ACV-honey drinks

Traditional use only
How to make & use
Chicken soup, ginger, horseradish, or a mug of 6 oz hot water with 1–2 Tbsp apple cider vinegar and 1 Tbsp honey (adults and older kids).
Tradition
Folk practice across many cultures. Apple cider vinegar drinks are popular in American folk medicine.
What the research says
Hot drinks and soup can temporarily loosen mucus and feel good. There are no trials of apple cider vinegar for sinus problems. [6] Sanu & Eccles 2008, Rhinology; [22] Rennard 2000, Chest

Safety & kids

  • Use only distilled, sterile, or boiled-and-cooled tap water for neti pots and rinses, never straight tap water. The FDA links tap-water rinses to rare but deadly amoeba infections.
  • Never use steam bowls or kettles with children, because scald burns are common. Use shower steam or a cool-mist humidifier instead.
  • Essential oils (eucalyptus, peppermint, camphor) should not be put on or near the faces of infants and small children, and must never be swallowed. Keep bottles out of reach.
  • Star anise tea is not safe for babies. Japanese star anise contamination has poisoned infants.

See a doctor when

  • Sinus symptoms lasting more than 10 days without improving, or a cold that gets better and then suddenly worse with new fever ("double sickening").
  • Severe headache, swelling or redness around the eye, vision changes, or a stiff neck (go now).
  • A baby who can't feed because of congestion, or any breathing difficulty.

Safety and red-flag sources: [23] U.S. FDA; [24] Poison Control (poison.org)
