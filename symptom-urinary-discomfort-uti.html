Urinary Tract Discomfort (bladder infection / UTI)

Burning when urinating, urgency, and needing to go often are classic bladder-infection symptoms. Home measures can ease discomfort and may help prevent repeat infections. An actual UTI usually needs a test, and often antibiotics, especially in children.

Traditional remedies

Plenty of water

Some evidence
How to make & use
Drink extra fluids to keep urine pale. Urinate when you feel the urge and after sex.
Tradition
Universal advice.
What the research says
NIDDK recommends fluids and these habits to help prevent bladder infections. [86] NIH NIDDK

Cranberry juice or capsules (prevention)

Good evidence
How to make & use
Unsweetened or low-sugar cranberry juice (about 8 oz a day) or standardized capsules.
Tradition
Native American and New England folk remedy.
What the research says
A 2023 Cochrane review found cranberry products reduce repeat symptomatic UTIs in women with recurrent UTIs, in children, and after urinary procedures. It does not treat an active infection. [87] Cochrane review; [88] NIH NCCIH

Warm heating pad

Traditional use only
How to make & use
A warm (not hot) pad on the lower belly for 15–20 minutes for comfort.
Tradition
Household care.
What the research says
A comfort measure only. [86] NIH NIDDK

D-mannose powder

Traditional use only
What they share
A 2019 customer review of the Woodstock store: "I told her I was looking for a natural supplement to help with my UTI and she said that D-Mannose powder is a great supplement to help with that and she helped show me where it was and also told me what her favorite brand of D-Mannose powder is."
How they suggest using it
No dose given in the review. Products commonly suggest about 2 g a day in water for prevention.
Store source
[89] Customer review, Nature's Corner Market Woodstock (Aug 31, 2019), via usarestaurants.info
What the research says
A 2022 Cochrane review found "little to no evidence to support or refute" D-mannose to prevent or treat UTIs. The largest trial (598 women, 2024) found it did not prevent recurrent UTIs. An earlier 2014 study was more positive. [90] Cochrane review; [91] Hayward 2024, JAMA Intern Med

Safety & kids

  • It is a sugar, so people with diabetes should monitor blood sugar. It can cause loose stools.
  • Not studied in children or pregnancy. A child, a pregnant woman, or a man with UTI symptoms should be seen by a clinician rather than self-treating.
  • D-mannose is not a treatment for an active kidney infection.

Safety & kids

  • Cranberry may interact with warfarin (NCCIH). Sweetened juice adds a lot of sugar.
  • Children with any UTI symptoms (or an unexplained fever) need a urine test. Kidney infections in young children can scar the kidneys.

See a doctor when

  • Any suspected UTI in a baby or child, during pregnancy, or in a man.
  • Fever, chills, back or side pain, nausea, or vomiting (possible kidney infection; same day).
  • Blood in the urine, or symptoms not improving within 1–2 days.
  • Sudden confusion or unusual behavior in an older adult. UTIs often show up this way.

Safety and red-flag sources: [92] AAP HealthyChildren; [86] NIH NIDDK
