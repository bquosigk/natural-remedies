Headache

Most headaches in children and adults come from tension, dehydration, missed meals, poor sleep, or a cold. Simple measures often help.

Traditional remedies

Water and a snack

Some evidence
How to make & use
Drink 1–2 glasses of water. Eat something if a meal was missed. Rest in a quiet, dim room.
Tradition
Universal.
What the research says
In one trial, people with recurrent headaches who drank more water reported better quality of life. Dehydration is a known trigger. [93] Spigt 2012, Family Practice; [94] NHS

Peppermint oil on the temples (adults and older kids)

Some evidence
How to make & use
Dilute 1–2 drops of peppermint oil in 1 tsp carrier oil (coconut, almond, or olive). Dab on the temples and forehead, away from the eyes. Reapply after 15 minutes if needed.
Tradition
Peppermint and menthol balms (like Tiger Balm) are traditional in Europe and Asia.
What the research says
German trials found 10% peppermint oil on the temples relieved tension headache about as well as acetaminophen. [95] Göbel 1996; [96] Göbel 2016, Schmerz

Cold pack, or ice on the head and warm feet

Traditional use only
How to make & use
Use a cold pack wrapped in a cloth on the forehead or the back of the neck for 15 minutes. One old trick puts ice on the head while the feet soak in warm water.
Tradition
Old household and hydrotherapy (water-cure) practice.
What the research says
Cold therapy is widely used for migraine with small supportive studies. The foot-bath method is untested. [94] NHS

Ginger

Some evidence
How to make & use
Ginger tea, or ⅛ tsp ginger powder in water at the start of a migraine (adults).
Tradition
Used in Ayurvedic and Chinese medicine.
What the research says
An adult trial found ginger powder was about as effective as sumatriptan for migraine, with fewer side effects. [97] Maghbooli 2014, Phytother Res

Acupressure (the "LI-4" point between thumb and index finger)

Traditional use only
How to make & use
Firmly squeeze the web between thumb and index finger for 1–5 minutes on each hand.
Tradition
Traditional Chinese medicine.
What the research says
There is limited, low-quality evidence. It is harmless, but avoid in pregnancy, where tradition holds it may stimulate labor. [94] NHS

Safety & kids

  • Peppermint oil must be diluted, kept away from the eyes, and never applied to the face of a baby or young child (menthol can cause breathing problems).
  • Watch for an overuse pattern: frequent headaches in a child are worth a doctor visit, whatever the remedy.

See a doctor when

  • The "worst headache of my life," a sudden thunderclap headache, or a headache with stiff neck, fever, rash, confusion, weakness, vision or speech changes, or after a head injury (call 911 or go to the ER).
  • Headaches that wake a child from sleep, come with morning vomiting, or are getting more frequent or severe.
  • A child who needs headache treatment more than twice a week.
