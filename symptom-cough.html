Cough

Coughs from colds often linger for 2–3 weeks. Home remedies mainly soothe the throat and help everyone sleep.

Top home remediesHoney, onion syrup, thyme tea, chest rub, humidifier
Best-supportedHoney (age 1+)
Key kid cautionNo honey under 1; no menthol rubs under 2
See a doctor ifLabored breathing; more than 3 weeks; baby under 3 months
RemediesCommunity notesSafety & kidsSee a doctor

Traditional remedies

Honey by the spoonful (age 1+)

Good evidence
How to make & use
Give at bedtime and as needed. AAP doses: ½ tsp for ages 1–5, 1 tsp for ages 6–11, 2 tsp for ages 12 and up. It can be given straight or in warm water.
Tradition
A cough remedy across many cultures. Now also recommended by pediatricians.
What the research says
This is the best-studied remedy here. A Cochrane review found honey probably relieves children's cough better than no treatment, diphenhydramine, or placebo, and about as well as dextromethorphan. The AAP recommends it for children over 1. [8] Cochrane review; [9] Paul 2007, Arch Pediatr Adolesc Med; [10] AAP HealthyChildren

Onion syrup (onion + honey or sugar)

Traditional use only
How to make & use
Slice 1 onion thin and layer it in a jar with honey (or sugar if a child is under 1). Cover and let it sit a few hours to overnight until a syrup forms. Strain. Give 1 tsp several times a day; adults can take 1 Tbsp. Refrigerate and use within a few days.
Tradition
Recorded in German, Eastern European, Hungarian, Indian, and American Southern families. Several People's Pharmacy readers describe mothers and grandmothers making it in the 1930s–50s.
What the research says
There are no clinical trials of onion syrup. Any benefit likely comes from the honey or sugar coating the throat. It is harmless for most people. [11] The People's Pharmacy; [12] OSF HealthCare

Thyme tea

Some evidence
How to make & use
Pour 1 cup boiling water over 1 tsp dried thyme. Steep 5–10 minutes, strain, and add honey (age 1+). Drink 2–3 cups a day.
Tradition
A European herbal cough remedy, and thyme is an ingredient in German commission-approved cough products.
What the research says
One adult trial found a thyme–ivy extract reduced coughing fits in bronchitis. The tea itself has not been tested. [13] Kemmerich 2006; [11] The People's Pharmacy

Vapor rub on chest and neck (age 2+)

Some evidence
How to make & use
Rub a thin layer on the chest and throat at bedtime. Never put it under the nose. Many families also rub it on the soles of the feet under socks.
Tradition
Menthol and camphor chest rubs date to the 1890s (Vicks was created by a North Carolina pharmacist). Older generations used mustard plasters and goose-grease rubs.
What the research says
In one trial, vapor rub on the chest improved children's night cough and parents' sleep more than petrolatum or nothing, though skin irritation was common. No studies cover rubbing it on feet. [14] Paul 2010, Pediatrics; [15] Abanses 2009, Chest

Propping up, humid air, and a cut onion in the room

Traditional use only
How to make & use
Raise the head of an older child's mattress slightly. Run a cool-mist humidifier and clean it daily. Some families leave a cut onion in the bedroom.
Tradition
The cut onion by the bed is a widespread European and British folk practice.
What the research says
Humidified air has mixed evidence. The cut onion has no studies behind it; it is harmless but smelly. [16] Cochrane review; [17] AAP HealthyChildren

Safety & kids

  • No honey under 12 months. For babies, use saline drops, suction, humid air, and extra feeds instead.
  • No camphor or menthol rubs under age 2. They can increase mucus and airway irritation in very young children, and camphor is toxic if swallowed.
  • The AAP advises against over-the-counter cough and cold medicines under age 6 and cough drops under age 4.
  • For babies under 1, keep the sleep surface flat and bare: no pillows, wedges, or blankets.

See a doctor when

  • Fast or labored breathing, ribs sucking in with each breath, wheezing, bluish lips, or a "whoop" after coughing fits (call 911 if severe).
  • Any cough with fever in a baby under 3 months.
  • A cough lasting more than 3 weeks, coughing up blood, or a cough that improves and then comes back worse with fever.
  • A barking, seal-like cough with noisy breathing in (croup). Cool night air or a steamy bathroom can ease it, but call if breathing is hard.

Safety and red-flag sources: [18] CDC; [19] CHEST expert panel 2017
