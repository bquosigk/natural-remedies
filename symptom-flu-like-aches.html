Flu-like Aches

Flu hits harder than a cold, with fever, body aches, and exhaustion. Rest and fluids are the core. For flu, NCCIH says no complementary approach has been proven to prevent it or relieve symptoms, so home care is about comfort. Antiviral medicine works best within 48 hours for high-risk people (young children, pregnancy, chronic illness).

Traditional remedies

Epsom salt bath

Traditional use only
How to make & use
Dissolve 1–2 cups Epsom salt in a warm bath (some adults use up to 4 cups). Soak 15–20 minutes.
Tradition
Named for the English town of Epsom, where the mineral spring was famous in the 1600s.
What the research says
Warm water eases aches. There is little evidence that magnesium absorbs through the skin. [110] Gröber 2017, Nutrients

Elderberry

Some evidence
How to make & use
See the syrup recipe in the Common Cold section. Some families give it several times a day at the first sign of flu.
Tradition
European folk remedy.
What the research says
Small trials (some industry-funded) reported faster recovery from flu symptoms. NCCIH says evidence is insufficient, and it is not a substitute for antivirals in high-risk people. [44] Hawkins 2019, Complement Ther Med; [46] NIH NCCIH

Ginger-lemon tea, bone broth, and chicken soup

Some evidence
How to make & use
Hot ginger tea with lemon and honey (age 1+), and broth-based soups. Sip throughout the day.
Tradition
Universal.
What the research says
Comfort and fluids, with some evidence that hot drinks relieve symptoms. [6] Sanu & Eccles 2008, Rhinology; [22] Rennard 2000, Chest

Oregano oil

Traditional use only
How to make & use
Typically sold as diluted capsules or drops for adults. Some put 1–2 drops in a carrier oil under the tongue.
Tradition
Mediterranean folk medicine. Popular in modern natural-health circles.
What the research says
There are lab and animal studies only (antimicrobial activity in test tubes) and no human trials for colds or flu. [111] Leyva-López 2017, Molecules

Safety & kids

  • Oregano oil is concentrated and can burn skin and mucous membranes. Don't give it to infants or young children, and avoid it in pregnancy. It may also interact with blood thinners.
  • Never give aspirin for flu-like illness to anyone under 18 (Reye syndrome).
  • Keep all essential oils locked up. A teaspoon swallowed can poison a child. Poison Control: 1-800-222-1222.

See a doctor when

  • Fast or troubled breathing, bluish lips, chest pain, or ribs pulling in (call 911).
  • Severe muscle pain (a child refuses to walk), dehydration, not alert, or seizures.
  • Fever above 104°F, or any fever in a baby under 12 weeks.
  • Fever or cough that improves and then comes back worse.
  • Anyone at high risk (under 5, pregnant, asthma, diabetes, heart disease, or over 65): call early, because antivirals work best within 48 hours.

Safety and red-flag sources: [18] CDC; [4] NIH NCCIH
