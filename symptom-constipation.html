Constipation

Constipation is very common in kids, especially during potty training, after starting solids, and when switching to cow's milk. Diet changes and routines usually fix it.

Traditional remedies

Prunes and the "P" fruits (prunes, pears, plums, peaches)

Good evidence
How to make & use
Adults: about 6–12 prunes (50 g) twice a day, or a glass of prune juice, warm or cold. Toddlers: 2–4 oz of prune or pear juice diluted with water, or puréed prunes. Babies over 6 months: 1–2 oz of 100% prune, pear, or apple juice a day (check with your pediatrician).
Tradition
Prunes and prune juice are an old American and European family remedy.
What the research says
In an adult trial, prunes worked better than psyllium fiber. Pediatric guidance lists these juices for constipated babies. [83] Attaluri 2011, Aliment Pharmacol Ther; [84] AAP HealthyChildren; [85] NIH NIDDK

Water, fiber, and movement

Good evidence
How to make & use
More water and fruits, vegetables, beans, oats, and whole grains. Less cheese, white bread, and milk (no more than 16–24 oz a day for toddlers). Daily active play.
Tradition
Universal advice.
What the research says
This is the mainstay recommended by NIH NIDDK. [85] NIH NIDDK

Warm bath and tummy massage

Traditional use only
How to make & use
Give the baby or child a warm bath. Then gently massage the belly clockwise and do "bicycle legs."
Tradition
Grandmother and midwife tradition.
What the research says
Comforting. There are few good trials. [84] AAP HealthyChildren

Toilet routine

Some evidence
How to make & use
Have the child sit on the toilet for 5–10 minutes after meals, with a footstool so the knees are above the hips.
Tradition
Standard pediatric advice.
What the research says
Recommended as part of first-line care. [85] NIH NIDDK

Olive oil and lemon water

Traditional use only
How to make & use
Adults: 1 Tbsp olive oil, or warm water with lemon, first thing in the morning.
Tradition
Mediterranean and Middle Eastern folk practice.
What the research says
There are no good trials. Low risk. [85] NIH NIDDK

Broad-spectrum digestive enzyme with betaine HCl

Traditional use only
What they share
In the guest talk on the store's channel, Dr. Bergeron suggested "a broad spectrum digestive enzyme with a little bit of betaine hydrochloride" for anyone with "digestive symptoms, gas, bloating… constipation, loose stools," to help absorb nutrients, taken with food.
How they suggest using it
With meals, per the label.
Store source
[54] Nature's Corner Market YouTube
What the research says
There are no good trials of over-the-counter enzyme or betaine HCl blends for constipation in the general population. [85] NIH NIDDK

Safety & kids

  • Not for children. Betaine HCl is an acid, so avoid it with reflux, ulcers, or while taking NSAIDs (ibuprofen, aspirin) or steroids. Constipation in kids responds best to fluids, fiber, and toilet routines.

Safety & kids

  • No honey or corn syrup for babies under 1, including old "Karo syrup" remedies.
  • Don't give mineral oil by mouth to infants (choking/aspiration risk), and don't give enemas or suppositories to babies without a doctor's advice.
  • Babies under 6 months: ask your pediatrician before giving juice.

See a doctor when

  • A newborn who hasn't passed stool in the first 48 hours, or a baby under 4 months who is constipated.
  • Constipation with vomiting, a swollen belly, weight loss, fever, or blood in the stool.
  • Lasting more than 2 weeks, or with pain, tears at the anus, or stool leaks in underwear.

Safety and red-flag sources: [84] AAP HealthyChildren
